The Life of Baybars

1223–1277 | Discover Islamic History

Click a topic below to discover his history.

Baybars was born around 1223 in the Kipchak steppe. He was captured and sold into slavery, eventually becoming a Mamluk soldier in the Ayyubid army. Through skill and leadership, he rose through the ranks to become a prominent commander.

In 1260, Baybars played a crucial role in the Battle of Ain Jalut. The Mamluks defeated the Mongol army, marking the first major halt to the Mongol expansion westward. This victory secured the survival of the Islamic world in the region.

After the assassination of Sultan Qutuz, Baybars took the throne in 1260. He established the Bahri dynasty and ruled for 17 years. He reorganized the army, built a strong navy, and established a postal system to govern his expanding empire effectively.

Baybars led numerous campaigns against the Crusader states and the Mongols. He captured key fortresses and secured the region. He died in 1277 in Damascus, leaving behind a legacy as one of the most powerful and successful Mamluk sultans.
